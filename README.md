# Formalization of boundedness of the trilinear Hilbert transform

[![Lean build](https://github.com/roos-j/lean-trilinearht/actions/workflows/lean_build.yml/badge.svg?branch=master)](https://github.com/roos-j/lean-trilinearht/actions/workflows/lean_build.yml)

This is a Lean 4 formalization of the `L³ × L³ × L³ → L¹` boundedness of the trilinear Hilbert transform.
The formalization follows a blueprint that was generated by GPT-6 Astra Pro on the basis of a preprint claiming this result [recently made public by OpenAI](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/An-L3-bound-for-the-trilinear-Hilbert-transform-October-5-2026/paper.pdf).
The preprint was made public without an accompanying formalization leaving possible doubts about its correctness. This formalization formally verifies its main result.

## Palomar

Palomar is a public, searchable registry of Lean formalizations whose proofs have been machine-checked. Each entry points to an immutable version of a public repository and records the exact formal statement that was checked, its dependencies, the result of the proof check, and the findings of a documented editorial review.

This formalization is registered in Palomar as [PALOMAR-2026-10-20-000001](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-20-000001).

## Autoformalization

The main theorem is stated in [`Theorems.lean`](TrilinearHT/Theorems.lean) with the operator defined in [`Defs.lean`](TrilinearHT/Defs.lean). These files are human-written. The proof of the theorem was entirely machine-generated. 

In the following we record some notes about the initial formalization of the main theorem which ran Oct 8-16. 

The formalization consists of about 194k lines of Lean code. The autoformalization process was started on October 8, 2026 initially using Codex/GPT-Astra 6 Light and Claude/Opus-5.5 Medium.
Claude at Medium reasoning effort eventually seems to have stalled. The agent did not disclose the issue and instead started avoiding the main difficulties and revisiting the same places in the blueprint over and over again, building more and more redundant 'bridges and wrappers', a common issue with large-scale autoformalization efforts. This was caught only through human monitoring of the `Status.md` which coding agents are instructed to maintain in the `autoformalize` skill.
After a large refactor using Claude/Opus-5.5 on Extra high the formalization was back on track and after a few more days finally finished the proof.

More detailed documentation of the formalization process can be found in the machine-generated files and logs in the `automation` folder.

The formalization closely follows the [machine-generated blueprint](blueprint/formalization_blueprint.tex) and thus also provides very strong evidence of overall correctness of the individual arguments contained in that blueprint, which in turn closely follow the original OpenAI manuscript. Discrepancies between blueprint and formalization have been documented by coding agents in `ErrorReport.md`. These discrepancies have not been human-reviewed and we note that they were written by agents of varying and limited reasoning capability (GPT-6 Astra Light/Opus-5.5 Medium and Extra high) none of which are as capable as GPT-Astra 6 Pro, or much less the as of this writing unreleased internal OpenAI model that is said to have produced the original manuscript.

### Token Usage

The formalization process took a total of about 8 full days during which at least one coding agent was always running. From October 8-10, formalization was done by Codex/GPT-6 Astra Light. This consumed an estimated 1.2B tokens total according to the author's ChatGPT account (the break up into individual categories is unclear).

Here is what Claude had to say after being asked about token usage (lightly edited AI output):

> What Claude actually consumed on this project over seven days (Oct 10–16), from the transcripts on disk:
>
> | Token type | Tokens | Share |
> |:-----------|-------:|------:|
> | Cache reads | 9.03 B | 99.1% |
> | Cache writes | 58.2 M | 0.6% |
> | Output | 26.4 M | 0.3% |
> | Uncached input | 0.04 M | ~0% |
> | **Total** | **≈ 9.1 B** |  |
>
> Volume: that is 17,717 requests, all to Opus 5.5, including subagents.
> Daily pace: on full days it ran at about 1.4–1.75 B tokens per day, almost all of it cache reads. 
> Leaving cache reads out, a full day was about 7–17 M cache-write plus 3–5 M output tokens.
> Why cache reads dominate: each request re-reads the long cached conversation context (typically 300–500k tokens), so total volume scales with request count times context size, not with how much new work was done.

In terms of subscription usage, the Oct 8-10 period used at least 80% of the total weekly Codex allowance of one GPT Pro 20x subscription (currently $200/mo), and the Oct 10-16 period used about 2x the total weekly allowance of one Claude Teams Premium seat (currently $125/mo or $15/mo as part of Claude for Scientists), but never reached the 5h usage limit. There were some periods with no or little activity when Claude was stalled.
